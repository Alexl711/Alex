FAQs

The YourPace program at the University of Maine at Presque Isle represents competency-based learning at its best, allowing you to progress through earning your undergraduate or graduate degree more efficiently, while focusing on the learning you need. Since not everybody is familiar with this approach, here are answers to frequently asked questions about YourPace at UMPI.

The UMPI YourPace program is an innovative, competency-based way for you to earn your degree on your terms. The programs are delivered in online, eight-week sessions, using a digital platform. Each course you take is built around learning modules with embedded competencies. You progress as you demonstrate mastery of each competency. Once you demonstrate mastery of all competencies and successfully complete your final assessment, you have completed the course. Since you determine how many courses to complete during each eight-week session, you have more control over your schedule and how quickly you progress toward completing your degree.

Yes, the YourPace program is accredited by The New England Commission of Higher Education (NECHE).

Applicants must be at least 20 years of age at the time of application, unless they have previously earned a bachelor’s or an associate’s degree from an accredited institution. Applicants must submit a clear copy of a valid, government-issued photo identification. Applicants must have a high school diploma or equivalent. Applicants must submit all official transcripts. They must have a minimum cumulative college GPA of 1.75 from the most recently attended institution. Visit the YourPace Admissions Requirements page for more information about admissions requirements.

To successfully participate in UMPI’s online programs, students must have access to appropriate technology and possess basic technical skills.

Required Device

  • A reliable desktop or laptop computer (Windows or Mac) capable of running standard productivity software and accessing online learning platforms
  • Chromebooks may allow for basic access, but they are not recommended as they may not support all required coursework activities
  • Smartphones and tablets are not sufficient as a primary device for coursework

Software & Course Tools

  • Microsoft Office 365 is provided at no cost to UMPI students and is commonly used for coursework

Internet Access

  • Reliable, high-speed internet access is required to participate in online courses, access learning materials, and submit assignments

Audio & Video Capabilities

  • A webcam and microphone are required for activities such as recorded presentations, voiceovers, and video-based assignments

Basic Technology Skills

Students should be able to:

  • Power on their device and connect to the internet
  • Navigate a web browser and access online platforms
  • Save files to their computer
  • Download and upload files such as PDFs, Word documents, and spreadsheets

There is no set limit to the number of credits you can transfer into UMPI. Transfer credits are evaluated to determine how they apply toward your chosen program and degree requirements.

However, students must still meet UMPI residency requirements in order to earn a degree:

  • Four-year degree programs (baccalaureate): Students must complete at least 30 credit hours through UMPI. At least 12 of these credits must be in the major, and at least 9 of those 12 credits must be at the upper level (300-level or above).
  • Two-year degree programs: Students must complete at least 15 credit hours through UMPI.

Transfer credit evaluations are generally applied to your academic record regardless of major; however, how those credits apply toward degree requirements may change if you change your major.

In competency-based education, courses are designed to help you build and demonstrate mastery of clearly defined skills and knowledge areas. Instead of focusing on seat time or lectures, you progress by showing what you know and can do.

Each course is structured around competencies, and learning is supported through curated course materials, activities, and practice opportunities that help you build understanding at your own pace. Courses include milestones, which are assignments such as discussions, quizzes, case studies, projects, or written work. These milestones help you apply what you are learning and prepare you for the final assessment.

At the end of the course, you complete a final assessment, which is the opportunity to demonstrate full mastery of the course competencies. This format is designed to support independent learning while still providing structure, feedback, and clear checkpoints throughout the course.

YourPace is a flat rate tuition program ($1,800 for undergraduate, $2,450 for graduate per eight-week session) and you can take as many courses as you like, as you progress at your own pace, during each session.

All of our undergraduate YourPace programs lead to a Bachelor’s degree, which requires a total of 120 credit hours. The graduate YourPace program leads to a Master’s degree, which requires a total of 36 credit hours.

There are six sessions offered each year with each session lasting eight weeks.

You can take as many courses as you can successfully complete within an eight-week session. Your advisor will work with you on scheduling to help ensure you are maximizing your time within the program.

Grades are based on the final exam. We utilize quizzes, practice questions, and interactive discussion questions for each objective within the course to help prepare you for the final. These exercises are not graded and can be taken as many times as needed to achieve mastery. Once you have completed each objective and take the final exam, a minimum score of 80 is required to pass the course. Learners will have the option to submit a draft of their final assessment prior to submitting their final exam.

  • A major is the primary focus of a student’s degree. It is the main field of study and determines most of the required coursework for earning the degree.
  • A minor is a secondary area of study that is optional. It requires fewer courses than a major and is used to complement or broaden a student’s main area of study. Our BLS and BAS majors require students to choose a minor; otherwise, a minor is optional.
  • A concentration is a specialized focus within a major. It allows students to tailor their major toward a specific area while still completing the core requirements of that major.

For those in UMPI’s YourPace program, the Educational Studies minor can provide foundational knowledge in education, but it is not a state-approved teacher preparation program. Students interested in certification must independently meet additional requirements, including completing a certified program or seeking alternative pathways approved by the Maine DOE.

Earning a Bachelor of Liberal Studies (BLS) degree with an Educational Studies Minor from UMPI can prepare you for roles in educational support or administrative settings, but it does not directly lead to teacher certification.

To become a certified teacher in Maine:

  • You must meet the Maine DOE’s certification requirements, which may include additional coursework, exams, or participation in a state-approved teacher preparation program.
  • Certification requirements vary by state, so if you plan to teach outside of Maine, check the requirements in your desired location.

While the Educational Studies minor is a valuable step toward a career in education, it does not guarantee certification. Students should work with advisors and review state-specific guidelines to ensure they meet all necessary criteria for teaching.

The primary difference between the Bachelor of Applied Science (BAS) and Bachelor of Liberal Studies (BLS) degrees lies in their focus and the type of student they are designed to serve:

  • Bachelor of Applied Science (BAS):
    • Designed for students with prior technical or applied coursework, such as those with an associate degree in an applied field.
    • Focuses on practical, career-oriented learning that builds on existing knowledge and skills.
    • Ideal for students seeking to advance in technical or applied professions with a bachelor’s degree.
  • Bachelor of Liberal Studies (BLS):
    • Offers a broad, interdisciplinary education across various fields of study.
    • Provides flexibility for students to customize their coursework based on their interests and career goals.
    • Suitable for students seeking a well-rounded education or exploring career paths in diverse industries.

Both degrees are part of the UMPI YourPace program, allowing students to complete their studies flexibly and efficiently. The choice between BAS and BLS depends on your prior educational background and professional aspirations.
